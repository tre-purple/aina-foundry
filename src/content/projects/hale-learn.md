## Overview

Reliable internet access is not a given in Hawaiian and Pacific classrooms. Schools on neighbor islands, in rural communities, and across Micronesia often operate with spotty or no broadband. Standard EdTech tools — which assume constant connectivity — fail these students.

Hale Learn is a Progressive Web App (PWA) that teachers install once and students use fully offline. Content syncs when connectivity is available; the learning experience never degrades when it isn't.

## What Makes It Different

**Culturally relevant curriculum** — Units are developed in collaboration with Hawaiian educators and community practitioners. Science units connect to ahupuaʻa, math to traditional navigation, engineering to traditional Hawaiian architecture and canoe building.

**Teacher-first design** — Teachers control what content is downloaded and available, can add their own modules, and see student progress without a cloud dashboard.

**Offline-first architecture** — Built with service workers and IndexedDB. Zero dependency on external servers during a lesson.

**Multi-language** — Interface available in ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi, Chuukese, Marshallese, and English.

## Venture Status

Hale Learn has graduated from prototype to an active venture. It is currently deployed in 14 classrooms across Oʻahu, Molokaʻi, and Chuuk State, Micronesia. We are in the process of establishing a separate nonprofit entity to sustain and scale operations.
