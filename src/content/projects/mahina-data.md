## Overview

Environmental data collected from and about indigenous lands has historically been extracted, repackaged, and published without meaningful consent or benefit-sharing with the communities that generated it.

Mahina Data is a platform that flips this model — putting data governance in the hands of the communities that create and hold environmental knowledge.

## The Problem with Current Tools

Standard data platforms (Google Sheets, ArcGIS, academic repositories) treat data as neutral assets with centralized admin control. They have no built-in mechanisms for:

- Expressing indigenous data protocols (when, how, and by whom data may be used)
- Community-defined metadata standards that reflect local ecological knowledge
- Consent workflows for data access requests
- Attribution that honors traditional ecological knowledge contributors

## What We're Building

Mahina Data provides:

- **Protocol layer** — attach Local Contexts Labels and CARE principle metadata to any dataset
- **Access control** — granular permissions from full public access to community-only, seasonal, or ceremony-restricted
- **API** — so sensor systems like WaiSense can push data directly into community-controlled storage
- **Export** — researchers can request access; communities can approve, deny, or conditionally share

## Research Phase

We're currently in deep research and co-design with three partner communities. We expect a prototype by mid-2025. This work builds on existing frameworks from Te Hiku Media (Aotearoa) and Local Contexts.
